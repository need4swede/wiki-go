# Devices

## One Neptune, to rule them all

Whatever device you pick up, you get the same home screen, the same search, the same Discover, the same [Conductor](/playback/conductor) picking your tracks, and the same [Trident](/playback/trident-player) engine playing your files. Sign in with the same account and your profile is your profile.

With the [Neptune MDM](/plugins/mdm) plugin installed, preferences sync live:
change your theme on one device and your other Neptune clients follow in real
time. Without it, each device keeps its own local settings.



## Shaped to the Screen

The interface adapts to how you actually hold and use each device:

| Device | How it feels |
|--------|--------------|
| **Apple TV** | Focus-driven, built for the Siri Remote: the Compass, spotlight carousel, and long-press menus |
| **iPhone** | Portrait-first with touch controls throughout, native [Pins](/ios/pins), configurable [widgets](/ios/widgets), an optional Compass [Live Activity](/ios/live-activity), and [Picture in Picture or background audio](/ios/playback) when you leave Neptune |
| **iPad** | The same touch experience with room to breathe, including native Pins, Home Screen widgets, Lock Screen widgets on iPadOS 17 or later, Picture in Picture, and background audio; the iPhone Compass and Live Activity stay off |
| **Mac** | TBD |

The documentation describes shared behavior with the TV controls as the reference. Where you read "press Select" or "long-press", the touch equivalent is a tap or a touch-and-hold.



## iOS System Surfaces

Neptune keeps three shortcut systems deliberately separate:

- **Native Pins** are cross-platform bookmarks for media, people, and library
  browse contexts.
- **Conventional widgets** work on iPhone and iPad. Every widget independently
  selects a Neptune Page, dynamic Section, or native Pin.
- **Compass Shortcuts** belong to the iPhone Compass palette and Compass Live
  Activity. They do not replace native Pins or widget selections.

[Picture in Picture and Background Playback](/ios/playback) are separate,
independent iPhone and iPad playback settings. One keeps video in a floating
system window; the other keeps audio playing through app switching and screen
lock.

See [iPhone & iPad](/ios) for the complete guide.
